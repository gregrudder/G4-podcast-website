“Should we film it?” is usually asked too late, in the studio, when the cameras are already in the room. The useful version of the question is asked when you are still choosing a booking.

## What audio is good at

An audio podcast is a conversation the listener can take on a commute, in a kitchen, or on a walk. The picture does not have to carry it. That is also the risk: if the recording is dull, there is nothing else to look at. Sound has to be clean. Mouth noises, a rattling table and two people talking at once are the whole experience.

Audio is the smaller production. Fewer cameras, a simpler edit, smaller files. It suits a show that already has listeners on Apple Podcasts, Spotify or a similar app, and that does not need clips for social platforms.

## What video changes

A video podcast is watched as well as heard. YouTube is the obvious home. It also gives you frames you can cut into vertical clips. The cost is not only the cameras. It is lighting, where people look, what is behind them, and an edit that cuts between shots instead of laying one waveform under a still image.

Multi-camera means the edit can move from one person to another instead of sitting on a single wide shot. That is how most conversation shows on YouTube are cut. It takes longer to edit than a single locked-off camera, and longer than audio. If you record three cameras and then publish audio only, you have paid for angles you will not use. That can still be the right call if you might want the picture later — say so, and keep the files.

## A practical way to choose

Answer these in order.

1. Where will a new listener find episode one? If the honest answer is YouTube, record video. If it is a podcast app, audio can be enough.
2. Do you need clips for TikTok, Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts? Those are pictures. Audio does not become a Reel without a visual, and a waveform animation is a poor substitute for a face.
3. Are the people comfortable on camera? A guest who will not be filmed is an audio booking, or a booking where that person is framed out. Decide before they travel.
4. Is the place part of the story? A desk, a couch, a shop floor or an event reads on video. On audio it does not, unless someone describes it.

You can record video and still publish an audio version. Many shows do both: the YouTube edit, and a stereo file for podcast apps. That is two exports, not two recording sessions. Put both in the brief if you want both. Do not assume an hourly studio rate includes either export.

## Sets, eyelines and lighting

On video, the background is part of the frame. A couch suits a loose conversation. A desk suits an interview or a business recording, because it gives people somewhere to put notes and it reads as a hosted show. G4 has both a couch set and a desk set in Wishaw. Which room has which set is confirmed when you book, not assumed from the name Studio 1 or Studio 2.

Lighting matters more than a camera badge. Flat overhead office light makes people look tired. A studio with dedicated lighting exists so you do not spend the first half hour moving lamps. G4’s studios use professional lighting and Sony ZV-E10 cameras. The ZV-E10 can record in 4K. That is a fact about the camera, not a promise that every file you receive will be a 4K master. Ask for the delivery format in the quote.

Eyeline is simple and often ignored. If two people are talking, they should be able to look at each other without staring down the lens unless the show is direct-to-camera. Tell the studio which you want.

## Clips are a separate decision

Choosing video does not automatically mean you have Shorts. A 45-minute episode and six 30-second vertical clips are different edits. Captions for clips are usually burned into the picture, because most people watch social video with the sound off at first. Say whether you want captions, and in which language. English captions for a Scottish show still need checked spellings of names and places.

Clip length, the number of clips, and whether they come from a G4 session or from files you already have should be in the quote. G4 prices that work per job. ## What to book

- Audio only: say so. You may still sit in a video studio, but you will not pay for an edit you do not want.
- Video, one publishable episode, no clips: book the room and quote a video edit.
- Video plus social clips: book the room, the edit and the clips as one brief, so the editor cuts them from the same selects.
