Most wasted studio hours are decided before anyone arrives. The room is ready. The microphones are ready. The running order is not, or four people have turned up when the booking was for two, or the logo file is a screenshot from Instagram.

This is the list we would want from any client, including people who never book [the Wishaw studios](/podcast-studio).

## Decide the format before you ask for a price

Audio only, one camera, or several cameras are different jobs. They change how long the room is needed, how the edit is priced, and whether you will have clips afterwards.

Write one sentence: “This episode will be published as ___.” If the answer is “a YouTube video and three vertical clips”, you are booking a video session plus an edit. If the answer is “an audio file for Apple and Spotify”, say that. A studio can record picture you never use, but you should know you are paying for it.

[Audio or video](/guides/audio-or-video-podcast) is a separate decision. Make it before you compare hourly rates. An hour that includes three cameras is not the same product as an hour with one microphone, even when the number on the rate card looks similar.

## Count the people, including yourself

Tell the studio:

- how many people will be in the room
- who is hosting
- whether anyone is joining remotely

Remote guests are a different setup. Ask the studio whether they can bring a caller in, and how the caller will hear the room. Do not assume a laptop on the table is enough. If the studio cannot do remote guests, you need to know before the day.

Also say if someone is only there for ten minutes. A guest who arrives halfway through still needs a microphone, a seat and, on a video show, a camera angle.

## Book more time than the finished episode

A 40-minute episode is rarely a 40-minute booking. People arrive, levels are checked, the intro is done twice, and someone tells a story you will cut.

Ask the studio how they like to pad a session. There is no universal ratio. A calm two-person interview might need half an hour on top of the finished length. A show with retakes, a scripted open, and three guests will need more. If you only book the length of the episode, you will either rush or overrun, and overrun may not be free.

## Write a running order on one page

It does not need to be a script. A useful page has:

- the title, or the working title
- the open: what the first minute is
- three to six beats, in order
- names, and how they are pronounced
- anything you must say (a sponsor line, a date, a correction)
- the close

Mark which stories are essential and which can be dropped if time goes. The editor, if you book one, will use the same page. There is a separate note on [how to brief an editor](/guides/how-to-brief-a-podcast-editor).

## Bring branding if the room has a screen

Some studios, including G4 in Wishaw, have a TV that can show your branding. Send a still or a logo ahead of the day, in a file the studio can actually open. A screenshot of a website, with a browser bar in the picture, usually looks like a screenshot.

Ask what size and format they want. If you do not have a logo, a plain background is better than a stretched image.

## Questions worth asking any studio

You can send these in one email. The answers tell you whether the hourly price is comparable.

1. What is included in the hour, and what is extra?
2. Is an engineer or producer in the room, or is the room self-service?
3. How are files delivered, and in what format?
4. Who owns the recording?
5. What is the cancellation rule, and is there a deposit?
6. Is there parking, and is the entrance step-free?
7. How many people can the room hold?
8. Can you record a remote guest?

If a studio cannot answer 1, 3 and 5 in writing, do not infer them from the name of a package. G4 publishes the hourly rates and what each hour includes. The booking email is still the spec for that job.

## What to do the day before

- Send the running order and the name spellings.
- Charge phones and then put them on silent. A phone on the table still buzzes into a microphone.
- Agree a hand signal for “stop, that was a false start” so you do not talk over each other.
- Bring water. Coffee cups move, and they hit desks on microphone.
- If you are reading, print the page or use a large font. A phone script pulls eyes down on camera.

## If it is your first time

Say so. A competent studio would rather explain the first five minutes than discover at the end that you thought the hour included a finished edit. You do not need your own microphones. You do need the people, the order of the conversation, and a clear note of what “done” means: raw files, an edited episode, or an edited episode plus clips.
